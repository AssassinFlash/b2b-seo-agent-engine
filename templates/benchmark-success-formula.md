## 4. Writer's Playbook (default recommendations for `/article-skeleton`)

> These are defaults, not mandates. Benchmark-native rhythm and `structure-variation` may override any recommendation when the reader's cognitive task demands a different format.

### 4.1 Paragraph contract: Why → How → Verify

Every section that teaches a decision or action may follow this three-part shape:

1. **Why (Context):** 1–2 sentences explaining why this section matters and what risk it addresses.
2. **How (Action):** The core instruction, comparison, or framework.
3. **Verify (Feedback):** The expected outcome, a check the reader can perform, or a concrete sign that the decision is correct.

### 4.2 Callout taxonomy

- **`[WARNING]`** — Safety, warranty, or compliance caveats that could cost the reader money or cause rework.
- **`[IMPORTANT]`** — Technical details essential for success that are easy to miss.
- **`[PRO TIP]`** — Practitioner advice framed as experience, not self-praise.

### 4.3 Visual contract

- One concept, one diagram/table for each decision framework.
- Product images when specific products are named.
- Comparison table when options are compared.

### 4.4 Expectation-setting opener block

Immediately after the hook, set expectations: what the reader will learn, what they should already know, and what decision they will be able to make.

### 4.5 Launchpad conclusion block

The conclusion should restate the core reframe, offer a single action item, and route to the next most relevant resource based on the reader's likely path.
