# Draft — Metal Room Divider Design Guide

A lobby designer in Austin walked into a 4,000-square-foot open floor plan and saw exactly one problem: the space needed to feel smaller without building walls. They chose a custom stainless steel room divider.

A metal divider is not just a screen. It is a way to create zones, filter light, and add texture while keeping the architecture open. For commercial interiors, that matters more than it does in residential work.

## Stainless steel grade choice shapes everything

Grade 304 and Grade 316 stainless steel each serve a distinct exposure boundary. Grade 304 handles climate-controlled commercial interiors cleanly, while Grade 316 adds molybdenum to prevent chloride pitting in coastal lobbies and humid spa corridors. **For high-traffic hospitality spaces, 3.0 mm Grade 304 or 316 stainless steel with vacuum PVD titanium-nitride coating is a safer long-term investment than thin painted substitutes.** The upfront specification is stricter, and the replacement cycle drops to zero. [T1-01]

## Design decisions

Pattern density controls privacy. Scale controls visual weight. Finish controls maintenance. Hairline brushed PVD finishes hide fingerprints far better than mirror #8 finishes in hospitality reception zones. [T1-03]

## Case in point

On a Toronto co-working project, our team fabricated fourteen champagne-gold PVD Grade 304 stainless steel panels across a 38-foot run. The pattern density was 40% open, which gave the space definition without blocking daylight. The install took two days. [T1-02]

## Specification checklist

- Confirm ceiling header and floor slab loads before locking frame thickness.
- Choose PVD finish texture (hairline brushed vs. bead-blasted) before pattern; finish drives daily maintenance.
- Request a physical stainless steel PVD sample at scale, not just a digital render.
- Plan mounting method: concealed ceiling U-channel, floor shoe, or full perimeter welded tube frame.

## When to choose stainless steel over glass

Glass keeps light but adds glare and daily smudges. Wood adds warmth but warps under commercial HVAC swings. Stainless steel sits in the sweet spot: rigid, cleanable, and visually architectural. When the space needs to read as engineered, a laser-cut stainless steel screen is the safer specification.
