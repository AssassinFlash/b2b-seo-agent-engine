# Regulatory & Policy Anchor Research

> Research window: 2026-06-23  
> Purpose: Supply fact-checked regulatory freshness anchors for the "Solar Without a Battery" article.  
> Risk rule: All tax/regulatory claims must be framed as "as of 2026 / confirm with a licensed professional," not advice.

## 1. U.S. National Electrical Code (NEC) 2023 — grid-tied solar without battery

- **Relevant articles:** Article 690 (Solar Photovoltaic Systems) and Article 705 (Interconnected Electric Power Production Sources).
- **Why it matters for the angle:** NEC 2023 governs how grid-tied inverters connect, rapid shutdown / arc-fault requirements, and grounding. A battery-less grid-tied system is simpler from a code standpoint because it avoids Article 706 (Energy Storage Systems) installation, permitting, and interconnection steps.
- **Claim we can use:** "NEC 2023 Article 690 still applies to any rooftop PV; add a battery and Article 706 joins the inspection checklist." Must attribute to NFPA and note local AHJ variations.
- **Sources:** NFPA 70-2023; NREL "SolarAPP+" permitting baseline; IREC interconnection guides.

## 2. U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) — residential clean energy credit

- **Current law as of 2026:** 30% federal tax credit for qualified solar electric property, including panels, inverters, wiring, and labor, through 2032.
- **Step-down schedule:** 26% in 2033, 22% in 2034; expires for residential installations after 2035 unless Congress extends.
- **Battery inclusion:** Standalone battery storage (not connected to solar) qualifies for the 30% credit for residential installations placed in service after 2022, with a capacity requirement of ≥3 kWh (IRC §25D).
- **Why it matters for the angle:** A homeowner can install grid-tied solar now, capture the 30% credit, and add a standalone battery later — still likely eligible for the credit on the battery if installed before the step-down or expiration. This supports the "battery-ready" financial strategy.
- **Claim we can use:** "As of 2026, the federal residential clean energy credit remains 30% for both solar and standalone storage, but the clock is ticking toward the 2033 step-down." Must not be presented as tax advice.
- **Sources:** IRS Form 5695 instructions; DSIRE database; White House clean energy fact sheets.

## 3. EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542) and Net Zero Industry Act

- **EU Battery Regulation:** Effective August 2023; mandatory battery passport for industrial and EV batteries ≥2 kWh from February 2027; due-diligence, carbon-footprint declarations, and recycled-content targets phased in through 2031.
- **Net Zero Industry Act:** Aims for 40% of strategic net-zero technologies (including solar PV and batteries) to be manufactured in the EU by 2030; streamlines permitting for manufacturing projects.
- **Why it matters for the angle:** Battery regulations are tightening faster than inverter-only rules. A battery-less solar system sidesteps the coming battery passport, carbon-footprint, and due-diligence compliance burden for the homeowner (though not for the installer/distributor).
- **Claim we can use:** "Starting February 2027, large EU batteries will need a digital passport. Grid-tied inverters face no equivalent passport requirement." Must attribute to EU official texts.
- **Sources:** EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2023/1542; European Commission Net Zero Industry Act Q&A; REA/Energy Storage Europe briefings.

## 4. Key framing guardrails for the article

- Do not tell readers they "don't need permits" for battery-less solar — NEC/local permitting still applies.
- Do not present tax-credit information as individualized advice; always add "confirm with a tax professional."
- When citing IRA step-down dates, use "scheduled to" or "as currently written" language.
- EU battery passport requirement applies to batteries ≥2 kWh from February 2027; do not overgeneralize to all storage systems.
