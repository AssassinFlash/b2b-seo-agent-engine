## Five borrowable techniques

### 1. 先说结论 (State the conclusion first)

- **What it is:** Open with the answer before the backstory. The first paragraph must give the reader the payoff.
- **Solar application:** "Yes, you can run solar without a battery. In fact, for about half of homeowners, it's the smarter first move. The battery can wait."
- **Where to use:** H1 intro, AIO answer block, conclusion lead-in.

### 2. Not X but Y (Reframe the reader's assumption)

- **What it is:** Take the common worry and flip it. The reader thinks they need a battery; the article says the real decision is "when," not "whether."
- **Solar application:** "The question isn't 'Do I need a battery?' It's 'Do I need a battery today?'"
- **Where to use:** Section opener after the intro, before comparison tables.

### 3. Protagonist + number (Anchor a claim in a specific story)

- **What it is:** Use a named or described person plus a concrete number to make the lesson stick.
- **Solar application:** "A homeowner in Norway installed a standard string inverter in 2020. Three years later, adding a battery cost $4,500 in retrofits. An SAJ H2 hybrid inverter bought in 2020 would have made that a $200 plug-and-play job."
- **Where to use:** Case-study paragraph, ROI proof section.

### 4. Claim → Evidence → Action (Three-beat reasoning)

- **What it is:** Every strong claim is followed by a fact and a practical next step.
- **Solar application:** "Hybrid inverters can run battery-free (claim). SAJ H2 models ship with the battery port inactive and behave like a grid-tied inverter until a battery is connected (evidence). If you're 90% sure you'll add storage within five years, buy the hybrid now and skip the retrofit later (action)."
- **Where to use:** Body sections, FAQ answers, decision-framework paragraphs.

### 5. Aphorism + artifact (Memorable one-liner plus a concrete object)

- **What it is:** End a section with a short, quotable line tied to a physical thing.
- **Solar application:** "The most expensive battery is the one you buy twice." Pair with a spec-table or a photo/diagram of an expandable inverter.
- **Where to use:** Section closers, social-share pull quotes, conclusion.

## Tone and pacing checklist

- Use short, declarative sentences. Avoid passive voice.
- Address the reader as "you." Speak directly.
- Allow one strong opinion per H2 section.
- Use rhetorical questions sparingly — only when they advance the argument.
- Replace generic transitions (e.g., broad claims about how quickly everything changes) with scene-setting or stakes.
- End sections with a takeaway the reader can quote or screenshot.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- AI clichés: generic openers about rapid change, vague claims that "solar is the future," and hyped labels like "revolutionary."
- Feature dumps without a reader decision.
- Generic listicles that could appear on any competitor site.
- Over-promising (e.g., "zero electricity bill forever").
