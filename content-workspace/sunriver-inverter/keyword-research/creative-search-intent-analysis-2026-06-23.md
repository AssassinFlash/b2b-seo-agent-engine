## Why the Obvious Angles Are Wrong

The surface-level recommendation would be to chase `solar inverter` (74k/mo) or `solar batteries` (74k/mo). But:

1. **Sunriver's blog already owns the hybrid/ESS conversation** — modern hybrid systems, all-in-one ESS comparisons, home battery specs, net metering, export limits, SAJ Elekeeper.
2. **Head terms are winner-take-most.** Ranking for `solar inverter` means beating EnergySage, SolarReviews, and Wikipedia on domain authority and topical depth.
3. **Generic guides do not travel.** They answer the question and die. They do not get shared in installer WhatsApp groups or quoted in distributor newsletters.

The real opportunity is in **counter-intent keywords** — searches where the user is asking the opposite of what the industry wants to sell them. These are low-KD, AIO-rich, and emotionally sticky.

### Why This Is a Sunriver Story

- Sunriver sells **on-grid inverters (R5/R6/C6)** that do exactly this: turn DC solar into AC power without any battery.
- Sunriver sells **hybrid inverters (H1/H2)** that can run in "battery-ready" mode — no battery required today, add one tomorrow.
- The existing article *"Can a Hybrid Inverter Work Without a Battery?"* proves the concept, but it is product-narrow. The broader "solar without battery" narrative is untouched.

### Proposed Article Hook

> **"The Battery Industry Hopes You Never Read This: Solar Works Without a Battery"**
>
> Open with a scene: an installer quotes $18,000 for panels + battery, and the homeowner asks, "What if I just want the panels?" The installer pauses — because the margin is in the battery.
>
> The article then walks through:
> 1. Three setups where no battery actually makes sense (grid-tied, battery-ready hybrid, backup-only).
> 2. The math: payback with vs. without storage in 2026.
> 3. The one case where skipping the battery is a mistake.
> 4. How Sunriver's on-grid and hybrid lines support both paths.
